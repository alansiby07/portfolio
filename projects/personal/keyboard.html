Custom Mechanical Keyboard

Designed and machined a 65% aluminum keyboard case with a gasket-mount PCB layout, modeling o-ring gasket tolerances to achieve a desired typing feel, then CNC-milling from 6061 billet.

Category Personal Project
Year 2023
Tools Fusion 360, CNC Mill, 3D Printer
Add photos or diagrams here

Overview

I've always wanted a keyboard that felt exactly the way I wanted — and couldn't find one. So I designed one. The goal was a gasket-mounted 65% layout in aluminum with a specific "thocky" low-pitched sound profile and medium typing flex.

Case Design

The case was modeled in Fusion 360. The gasket mount uses 10 Shore 40A o-rings seated in pockets that allow the PCB tray to float ±0.3mm vertically. Getting the o-ring groove dimensions right required referencing Parker o-ring design guidelines and a bit of trial and error with 3D printed prototypes.

Tolerancing

The tightest tolerance was the PCB tray fit: too tight and the gaskets bottom out, too loose and the board rattles. I modeled the tolerance stack-up and targeted a 0.1mm clearance on each side. The machined parts came out within 0.05mm of nominal.

Machining

The case halves were machined from 6061-T6 aluminum billet on a Tormach PCNC440 at my university's makerspace. I wrote the CAM toolpaths in Fusion 360. Total machining time was about 4 hours per half.

Result

The finished board sounds and feels exactly as intended. The gasket mount gives it a soft, bouncy bottom-out that's very different from a standard tray mount. Total cost was around $180 including PCB and switches.

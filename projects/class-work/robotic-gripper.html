Mechanism Design

Robotic Gripper Assembly

Designed a four-bar linkage gripper mechanism targeting sub-millimeter repeatability for a simulated surgical robotics use case, with full assembly drawings and tolerance stack-up analysis.

Category Semester Project — Machine Design
Year 2023
Tools SolidWorks, MATLAB
Add photos or diagrams here

Overview

For Machine Design (ME 4210), I designed a compliant parallel-jaw gripper actuated by a four-bar linkage. The target application was a minimally invasive surgical robot where precision and compactness are critical.

Kinematic Synthesis

I used Freudenstein's equation in MATLAB to synthesize a four-bar linkage that produced a near-linear jaw closing motion over a 15mm travel range. Multiple candidate solutions were evaluated for transmission angle and mechanical advantage.

Tolerance Stack-Up

With a repeatability target of ±0.1mm at the jaw tips, I performed a worst-case and RSS tolerance stack-up analysis on the full kinematic chain. This drove tighter tolerances on the coupler link pivot holes and necessitated specifying a precision bearing at the crank pivot.

SolidWorks Assembly

The full assembly was modeled in SolidWorks with all mating conditions applied. I verified the kinematic motion using SolidWorks Motion and generated a full drawing package including an assembly drawing, BOM, and detail drawings for each machined component.

Lessons Learned

This project taught me how quickly tolerances compound in a multi-link mechanism. The RSS analysis was much more realistic than worst-case, and it was a valuable exercise in deciding where to spend the tolerance budget.

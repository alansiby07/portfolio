Thermal Systems

Compact Heat Exchanger Design

Designed and iteratively optimized a cross-flow heat exchanger for a portable industrial unit, achieving a 22% reduction in thermal resistance over the baseline configuration.

Category Course Project — Heat Transfer
Year 2024
Tools ANSYS Fluent, SolidWorks
Add photos or diagrams here

Overview

This was my final project for Heat Transfer (ME 3650). The goal was to design a compact cross-flow heat exchanger that met a target thermal performance within a constrained envelope. I started from first principles — calculating required UA values, estimating NTU-effectiveness, and sizing the core — then refined the geometry using CFD.

Problem Statement

The design brief called for a heat exchanger capable of cooling a 5 kW heat load using ambient air, packaged within a 200×200×100 mm envelope. The primary constraint was minimizing pressure drop on the air side while maximizing heat transfer.

Design Process

I began with an analytical sizing using the ε-NTU method, then modeled three fin geometries in SolidWorks — plain fins, offset strip fins, and louvered fins. Each was exported to ANSYS Fluent for conjugate heat transfer simulation under identical boundary conditions.

Results

The louvered fin geometry outperformed the others, reducing thermal resistance by 22% compared to the plain fin baseline while keeping air-side pressure drop within the 50 Pa limit. The final design was documented with full GD&T drawings.

What I Learned

This project gave me my first real exposure to CFD post-processing and the importance of mesh independence studies. I also learned how much the analytical predictions can deviate from simulation when entrance and exit effects are significant.

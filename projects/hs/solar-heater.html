Science Fair

Passive Solar Water Heater

Built and tested a low-cost thermosiphon solar collector, evaluating panel geometry and insulation materials and achieving a 41°C temperature rise under test conditions.

Category High School — Regional Science Fair
Year 2020
Tools Handmade Prototype, Thermocouple Data Logger
Add photos or diagrams here

Overview

For my sophomore science fair project I built a passive solar water heater from scratch using materials sourced from a hardware store for under $40. The goal was to evaluate whether a simple thermosiphon system could provide meaningful water heating for a household in a sunny climate.

Design

The collector consisted of a copper pipe serpentine bonded to a painted aluminum absorber plate, enclosed in a wood-framed box with a polycarbonate glazing panel. The thermosiphon loop connected the collector to an insulated storage tank elevated above it.

Testing

I instrumented the system with six type-K thermocouples at the inlet, outlet, tank, and ambient positions, logging data every 30 seconds on a Arduino-based datalogger I built. Tests were run over 5 identical solar days.

Results

Peak temperature rise of 41°C above ambient was measured at solar noon. I also tested three insulation materials (fiberglass batt, foam board, and reflective bubble wrap) on the tank and found foam board outperformed the others by 18% over an 8-hour period.

Awards

The project placed 2nd at the regional fair in the Physics & Engineering category. The judge feedback noted the quality of the experimental controls and data logging approach.

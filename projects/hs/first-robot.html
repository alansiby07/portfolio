FIRST Robotics Competition Robot

Led mechanical design on a 120 lb FRC competition robot, designing a 4-bar ball intake mechanism and belt-driven shooter assembly fabricated on a CNC router and lathe.

Category High School — FIRST Robotics
Year 2021
Tools Onshape, CNC Router, Metal Lathe

Overview

In my junior year of high school I was the lead mechanical designer on our FIRST Robotics Competition team. The 2021 game required robots to collect and shoot foam balls into elevated targets. I designed the two main mechanisms: the ground intake and the flywheel shooter.

Intake Mechanism

The intake used a 4-bar linkage to deploy a compliant roller from inside the robot frame to the ground. I sized the rollers and chose 30A durometer polyurethane to maximize grip on the foam balls without crushing them. The linkage geometry was iterated through cardboard prototypes before finalizing in Onshape.

Flywheel Shooter

The shooter used dual 4-inch compliant wheels driven by two NEO motors through a 1:1 belt reduction. I designed the hood geometry by testing different exit angles with a simple spreadsheet ballistics model, targeting a 3m shot distance at 45° elevation.

Fabrication

All aluminum structural components were cut on our team's CNC router from 1/8" and 1/4" 6061 plate. I learned to program toolpaths in Fusion 360 CAM and ran the machine myself. The main shaft for the shooter was turned on a manual lathe.

Competition Results

The robot performed reliably at our regional event. The intake had a 92% pick-up success rate in practice matches. This experience is what made me decide to pursue mechanical engineering.

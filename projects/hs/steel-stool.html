Shop Class

Welded Steel Stool

Designed and fabricated a load-bearing steel stool from square stock using MIG welding and angle grinding, applying basic stress calculations to verify weld joint adequacy.

Category High School — Metal Shop
Year 2019
Tools MIG Welder, Angle Grinder, Manual Mill
Add photos or diagrams here

Overview

In my freshman year metal shop class I designed and built a four-legged steel stool entirely from 1" square steel tube. It was my first real fabrication project and my first attempt at applying engineering math to something I was actually going to build.

Design & Calculations

Before cutting any metal I sketched the design and did basic beam bending and weld shear calculations by hand to verify the leg joints could support a 300 lb load with a safety factor of at least 3. The math was simple but it was the first time I felt like the equations meant something real.

Fabrication

I cut all pieces on the horizontal band saw, squared the ends on the manual mill, and tack-welded the assembly in a fixture before running final welds. The shop teacher taught me to walk the puddle on the MIG welder to get consistent penetration on the lap joints.

Finishing

After grinding the welds flush I cleaned the steel with acetone and applied two coats of matte black Rustoleum. I cut a circular seat from 3/4" plywood and upholstered it with vinyl and foam.

Reflection

This was the project that got me interested in manufacturing. Seeing the direct connection between a sketch, some math, and a physical object you can sit on was the moment engineering clicked for me.
